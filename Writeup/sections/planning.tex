\section{Project planning}
\label{sec:jesper-planning}
\contributionauthor{Jesper Eggers}
Each group member primarily owns their report sections. The project maintains
two agent implementations and a staged training workflow: transfer from saved
weights, alternate training and greedy evaluation, then archive the
best model and parameter snapshot. Training logs record two
30,000-round RUEHL continuation stages, lasting 8:47:59 and 8:03:33.
Their 180/20 schedule nominally allocates 27,000 rounds to training and
3,000 to evaluation per stage; these are played-round budgets, not counts
of optimization updates.

The final evaluation stage freezes the final network and varies its
action-selection components. This separates model development from
assessing what each deployed component contributes, while keeping the
evaluation workload finite before the report deadline. The paired-game
design gives each configuration the same initial conditions, and the
analysis connects game scores to survival and action-selection behavior. The
organizational lesson is to preserve source revisions, model hashes,
per-episode evaluations, and hardware records together at each milestone.
